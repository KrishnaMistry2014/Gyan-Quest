# Bhagavad Gita for Students
## Become the Architect of Your Own Success Through Timeless Ancient Wisdom

### Chapter 6: Dhyana Yoga - Verse 6.17

Have you ever felt too busy or cluttered with homework, projects, classes, or studies? Let’s say, just like a computer, which has multiple tabs open and is running too slow. Don’t worry, because it happens to everyone. 

Timeless ancient wisdom from India, revealed about 5000 years ago in the holy Bhagavad Gita, will help you find a solution to this problem and also find success in life. Verse 6.17 has a major importance in a student’s life, as it helps understand the importance of prioritization, how to do it, and how to complete work properly without rushing.

> *yuktahara-viharasya yukta-cestasya karmasu*
> *yukta-swapnavabodhasya yogo bhavti duhka-ha*

> *"He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation, and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."*

---

### Word-by-Word Breakdown

- **yukta** — regulated
- **ahara** — eating
- **viharasya** — recreation
- **yukta** — regulated
- **cestasya** — of one who works for maintenance
- **karmasu** — in discharging duties
- **yukta** — regulated
- **swapna-avbodhasya** — sleep and wakefulness
- **yogo** — practice of yoga
- **bhavati** — becomes
- **duhkha-ha** — diminishing pains

---

### Meaning & Application

This means that if a person performs their daily activities and needs (like eating, sleeping, working, and recreation) properly with full dedication, they will have no problem in life. Let’s explore how in the following sections.

#### 1. Regulated Resting
Once you get too much homework and have to finish and submit it to the teacher the next day, what is your first instinct? Maybe stay up all night and complete it. Most of you would do the same. You might have heard the old saying: *“Early to bed, early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy, and wise.”*

Every average human being needs a specific amount of sleep according to their age:
- **Kids (6–13 years):** 9–12 hours of night sleep.
- **Teenagers:** 8–10 hours of night sleep.

When a person sleeps more than recommended, they may experience headaches, increased risk of health problems, slower reaction time, poor short-term memory, and reduced clarity of thought. 

When a person sleeps less than recommended, they experience daytime sleepiness, difficulty concentrating, slower reaction times, and mood changes, leading to increased risk of accidents and injuries.

#### 2. Regulated Eating
Imagine you are home alone, feeling hungry, and there is no cooked food. What might you do? You might grab a packet of instant noodles or junk food. Why junk food instead of healthy food? The answer is simple: junk food is designed to be instantly palatable through added flavorings and chemicals that encourage repeat purchases while harming our bodies.

Our body is like a machine:
- **Healthy food** fuels the machine, providing energy to work faster and productively.
- **Junk food** acts like moisture/rust on a metal machine, causing it to degrade quickly.

Every meal should include nutrients: vitamins, minerals, proteins, carbohydrates, fats, and roughage. Fats should be kept to a minimum but consumed. A food plate should be colorful with a variety of vegetables, fruits, pulses, and grains. Eating should be timely (three meals a day, with dinner about 2 hours before bedtime).

#### 3. Staying Active
*“A healthy mind resides only in a healthy body.”* This old saying emphasizes physical activity such as walking, running, cycling, swimming, yoga, or sports at least 4 days a week.

**Consequences of not exercising:**
- **Muscle Atrophy:** Muscles weaken and shrink due to disuse.
- **Metabolic Slowdown:** Harder to burn calories, leading to weight gain.
- **Increased Disease Risk:** Higher risk of heart disease, diabetes, and obesity.
- **Mental Health Decline:** Increased stress and anxiety.
- **Cognitive Decline:** Poor regulation of neurotransmitters controlling mood.

**Consequences of over-exercising:**
Persistent muscle soreness, injury risk, extreme fatigue, weakened immune system, and overtraining syndrome (pain, stiffness, weight changes, anxiety, poor sleep).

#### 4. Regulated Work
The art of working properly means completing tasks with full focus and devotion, similar to a *Sankalp* before a *Yajna*. We should maintain balance by doing everything on time and in moderation so the mind stays calm. 

For example, completing homework correctly with breaks avoids last-minute stress and poor quality. To cultivate devotion: sit quietly, understand why you are doing the task, and recognize both immediate impulses (e.g., wanting to play) and long-term consequences (knowledge, academic success, peace of mind).

#### 5. Recreation for Our Mind
*“All work and no play makes Jack a dull boy.”* Our mind tires when doing the same task for too long, leading to distraction. Breaks prevent burnout so we return fresh. Recreational activities include singing, painting, swimming, dancing, and hobbies. However, excessive recreation disrupts productivity, so balance is key.

#### 6. Habit Formation
If you still feel tired while working, it may be due to a lack of established habits. Doing the same task at the same time in the same manner for **21 days or more** forms a habit. Plan your day each morning with a mind map and follow it consistently for 21 days.

### Conclusion
Implementing these principles naturally improves gratitude, consciousness, attention span, memory, focus, and recall.
